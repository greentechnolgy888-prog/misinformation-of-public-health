What if a health post had to prove itself before it went live?

Most platforms deal with false health claims after people have already seen them. We’re exploring the opposite: an AI check that runs at the moment someone presses Share.

The tool on the right is a rough prototype of that moment. Pick a case and press Share.

Example only, to show how the product would work. The cancer claims are false on purpose, and the scores are simulated.

How it works

  1. The publisher presses Share. Before the post goes out, three boxes appear.
  2. They fill in the topic, the tags and the source. For the cancer example: the daily steps being claimed, tags like Cancer, Treatment and Medical Advice, and the organization the claim comes from.
  3. The AI compares three things: what the post says, what the attached source says, and what an updated database holds on that health topic.
  4. It gives a match percentage between the source and the content. If something doesn’t add up, the post is blocked.

When it stops a post

The warning says: “Please double-check the attachments before posting.” Posting stays locked until the information is corrected or the post is deleted. It appears when:

  • the source has a low reliability rate,
  • the post and the source say different things,
  • the source doesn’t exist,
  • the database disagrees with the source or the post, or
  • the topic, tags or source box is empty.

Why we’re doing this

Our field is misinformation and AI in public health. Claims about treatment, cancer especially, can push people away from real care, and by the time a fact-check arrives the post has usually travelled.

This is a concept, not a working product. We still have to work out who maintains the database, how a source gets its reliability rating, and what a publisher can do when the AI gets it wrong.

A conceptual project. Nothing here is medical advice.
